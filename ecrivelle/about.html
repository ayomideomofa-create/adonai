Every book starts as something fragile: a voice memo, a folder of notes, a draft that almost works. My job is to take that fragile thing seriously, and help it become the book its author imagined.

I work across the whole life of a manuscript. Some clients come to me with an idea and need it written. Others arrive with a finished draft that needs a structural rethink, a careful line edit or a final proofread. Many need their book formatted for Amazon KDP, IngramSpark or an ebook store. Because I do all of it, nothing gets lost between stages.

I write and edit both fiction and nonfiction — memoir, self-help, leadership and business books, faith-based writing, novels and stories for younger readers. Whatever the genre, the question I keep asking is the same: what does the reader need on this page?

Your voice stays yours. My work is to make it clearer, stronger and ready to be read.

My approach

Writing philosophy

Good writing is invisible. Readers shouldn't notice the sentences; they should notice the story, the argument, the feeling. That means editing is often about removal: cutting the throat-clearing, the repeated point, the word that dilutes the one next to it.

I start every project by understanding who will read the book and what they should feel or do when they finish it. Every decision — structure, tone, word choice, even margin size — answers to that.
